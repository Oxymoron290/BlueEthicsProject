# City of Bedford, Texas — Election Cost Analysis (2019–2025)

*Analysis based on records obtained via Texas Public Information Act (Chapter 552) request*
*All scanned/image-based PDFs processed via Tesseract OCR v5.4.0*

---

## Executive Summary

Between 2019 and 2025, the City of Bedford conducted **8 completed elections** (5 general, 3 special/proposition) at a **total verified cost of at least $107,849** in Tarrant County administration fees alone. When adding Star-Telegram legal notice publication, translation services, and the 2020 liquor petition verification, the **total documented election spending reaches approximately $126,755**.

The single largest cost driver is the **cost-sharing multiplier** assigned by Tarrant County, which ranges from **0.0019 (0.19%)** in large joint elections to **0.5000 (50%)** in small standalone special elections — a **263× difference**. This multiplier, more than any other factor, determines what Bedford pays.

**Critical finding:** The August 2022 special election cost **$24,674** at a 50% multiplier (shared only with Burleson ISD), while the November 2020 proposition cost just **$80** by piggybacking on the presidential election. Both were single-issue elections — the difference in cost-sharing produced a **308× cost difference**.

## 3. Cost Analysis

### 3.1 Tarrant County Cost Allocation Methodology

Bedford's election costs are determined through a **joint election cost-sharing model** administered by Tarrant County Elections. Key mechanics:

- **Multiplier**: Each entity receives a multiplier (percentage of total costs) based on the number of registered voters and contests on the ballot
- **Tab number**: Entities are assigned a "Tab" number for billing purposes — Bedford has been Tab 5, 6, 7, or 8 depending on the election
- **10% administrative fee**: Tarrant County adds a 10% surcharge on top of all election expenses
- **75% deposit**: The 2025 contract specifies entities must deposit approximately 75% of estimated costs in advance
- **Cost categories**: Expenses are divided into Early Voting by Personal Appearance (EVPA), Early Voting by Mail (EVM), Election Day (ED), and General Expenses

### 3.2 The Multiplier Effect — Why Costs Vary So Dramatically

The multiplier is the single most important factor in determining election cost:

| Election | Multiplier | Bedford Cost | Entities Sharing |
|----------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| May 2021 General | **0.0019** (0.19%) | $5,313.74 | 30+ (large joint election) |
| May 2024 General | **0.0027** (0.27%) | $10,187.34 | 39 entities listed in contract |
| May 2022 General | **0.0030** (0.30%) | $11,301.36 | 35+ entities |
| May 2025 General | **0.0120** (1.20%) | $20,379.30 | ~20 entities⁶ |
| Aug 2022 Special | **0.5000** (50.0%) | $24,674.18 | **2 entities** (Bedford + Burleson ISD) |

The 2022 special election multiplier of **0.5000** is extraordinary — Bedford paid **half of all county election expenses** because only Burleson ISD shared the election. Compare this to the 2021 general where Bedford paid just 0.19% due to 30+ entities splitting costs.

## 5. Structural Observations

### 5.1 The Cost-Sharing Multiplier Is Everything

The multiplier is the dominant cost driver, dwarfing all other factors:

| Multiplier | Context | Bedford Cost | Cost per Bedford Voter |
|-----------|---------|-------------|----------------------|
| 0.0019 | 30+ entity joint election (2021) | $5,314 | $0.16 |
| 0.0027 | 39 entity joint election (2024) | $10,187 | $0.31 |
| 0.0030 | 35+ entity joint election (2022) | $11,301 | $0.34 |
| 0.0120 | ~20 entity election (2025) | $20,379 | $0.62 |
| 0.5000 | **2 entity** special election (2022) | $24,674 | $0.74 |

**The number of entities participating in the joint election is the primary determinant of cost.** The same county infrastructure runs regardless — the question is how many ways it's split.

### 5.2 The Actual vs. Estimated Cost Problem

The 2025 contract estimated Bedford's cost at $15,627.41, but the actual invoice was $20,379.30 — a **30% overrun**. This is consistent with a pattern:

| Election | Estimated Cost | Actual Cost | Variance |
|----------|---------------|-------------|----------|
| 2020 Liquor Petition | $2,738.70 | $3,395.18 | +24% |
| 2021 Special | $18,000.00 | $24,162.99 | +34% |
| 2022 General | $9,778.06 | $11,301.36 | +16% |
| 2025 General | $15,627.41 | $20,379.30 | +30% |

Estimates consistently run **16–34% below actuals**, meaning the City should budget at least 30–35% above the contracted estimate.

### 5.3 Special Elections Are Expensive and Low-Turnout

| Metric | General Elections (avg) | Special Elections (avg) |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Tarrant County cost | $5,300–$20,400 | $24,163–$24,674 |
| Star-Telegram cost | $675–$6,236 | $1,543.50 |
| Voter turnout | 6–13% | 5% |
| Cost per ballot | $2–$7 | $15–$16 |

Both standalone special elections (Aug 2021 Place 4, Aug 2022 Place 1) were triggered by **mid-term council vacancies**. The city could have saved approximately **$50,000** over this period by aligning vacancy elections with scheduled general elections.

## 7. Conclusions

### 7.1 Key Findings
